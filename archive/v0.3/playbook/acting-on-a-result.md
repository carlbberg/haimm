# Acting on a result

An assessment that names a gap and offers no next move is half a tool. This is the other half.

Nothing here is validated against practice. The profiles below are reasoned from the model's own structure, not observed in a population. Treat them as a way to read a result, not as findings.

## The one rule

**Work the failed criterion, not the stage.**

Gate criteria are written as states that can be inspected, which means each one already contains its own next move. A team held at a gate on "every context artifact the team relies on has a named owner and a last substantive change inside an agreed freshness window" does not have a Knowledge & Context problem to solve. It has a list of artifacts with no owner, and the task is to give them one.

"Get to Integration" is not a plan. "These four documents have no owner and none has changed in eight months" is.

## Sequencing

**One gate at a time, across the whole profile.** Not one gate per dimension in parallel. A team that tries to move five dimensions at once moves none, and the dimensions do not need to move together.

**Pick the gate by consequence, not by depth.** The lowest-scoring dimension is not automatically the one to work. Ask which failure is currently costing the team something, and which is compounding. A dimension sitting at Exploration that nothing depends on yet can wait; one at Integration that everything routes through cannot.

**Reassess after a cycle, not continuously.** Gates measure states that take a quarter or more to change. Running the instrument monthly measures the assessor's mood.

## Reading the shape

Six profiles worth recognising. The pairwise ones matter more than any single dimension's level.

**Solution Fit ahead, Knowledge & Context behind.** The tools fit the work and the grounding lives in a few heads. Usually presents as "our pilot worked and the rollout stalled". The move is transferability: document what made the pilot work before scaling anything else. Attacking it as a training or tooling problem is the standard and expensive mistake.

**Human-AI Collaboration ahead of Knowledge & Context.** Trust has outrun grounding. The team treats AI as a colleague and re-briefs it every session, which is expensive and invisible because it feels like normal work. This is the gap the row order was chosen to make visible, since the two dimensions are adjacent in the assessment view. The move is reachability, not more trust.

**People behind everything else.** Capability is concentrated. One or two people carry the AI-supported workflow and the profile looks healthy because they are the ones who got interviewed. The move is distribution: onboarding material, and a named person who is not the original builder running the workflow unaided.

**Ethics behind everything else.** The risk compounds with automation level, so this profile gets worse without anything changing. A team at Optimization on Human-AI Collaboration and Exploration on Ethics has semi-autonomous operation with unfalsifiable guardrails. The move is one specific guardrail that names an action it prohibits, applied to the highest-automation use case in play.

**Flat and low.** All five at Exploration or Experimentation. This is the normal state of a team that started recently and is not a crisis. Work Solution Fit and Knowledge & Context first; the other three have less to bite on until there is something real in use.

**Flat and high.** All five at Optimization or above. Check the evidence before believing it. A uniformly high profile from a survey-led assessment with thin artifact access is the instrument's most likely false positive, and the failure signals are the check: a team that cannot produce a single grounding failure, a pilot that never changed, an ethics checklist that never blocked anything.

## What not to do

- **Do not level the profile.** Even placement across five dimensions is not a goal. The scatter is information, and a team that flattens it has usually optimised the assessment rather than the work.
- **Do not treat the stages as a roadmap with dates.** They describe states, not a schedule. A team can sit at Integration on a dimension for years because that is the right place for it.
- **Do not publish the criteria to the team before assessing them.** Published criteria get optimised against. If they are shared, share them after placement, as the description of what to do next, which is what they are good for.
- **Do not convert the profile into a number for reporting upward.** If a single figure is demanded, give the gate that is blocking and what it would take to pass it. That is shorter than a number and means something.
