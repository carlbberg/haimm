# Continuous Evolution

*Stage 5 of five*

**Ongoing adaptation as organisational needs and technologies change.**

The one stage in the model that has never been observed. Everything below should be read with that in front of it.

## Status

No assessment has placed a team at this stage on any dimension. The four gates leading into it, one per dimension, are reasoned from the stages before them rather than checked against a team that passed them. Each of the five dimension files says so at that gate, `playbook/running-an-assessment.md` repeats it under known weaknesses, and it remains the single largest untested part of the model.

This is not a hedge attached to an otherwise finished stage. It is the reason to be sceptical of any assessment that places a team here.

## What is happening

Need is re-checked on a cadence rather than assumed stable. Context is managed with ownership, refresh and deprecation. Guardrails get revised because something new arrived, not because a review was scheduled. The record shows things being narrowed and retired, not only added.

The distinguishing evidence is subtraction. Every earlier stage can be evidenced by things being built. This one asks what was taken away, because a team that only adds is expanding, and expansion is what capability does on its own.

## What it is not

- **Not the end.** The name is the argument. A model whose final stage is a resting point would describe adoption of a technology that has stopped changing.
- **Not the accumulation of AI capability.** Each new model and feature adopted as it ships, with no corresponding record of a need being re-checked, is frontier drift. It produces a convincing appearance of evolution and no evidence of any.
- **Not a level anyone should be eager to award.** Given that nothing here has been observed, a confident placement at this stage says more about the assessment than the team.

## How the lenses read it

Both borrowed developmental lenses run out, and neither failure is quiet.

Tuckman and Jensen (1977) added adjourning as a fifth stage, meaning the group disbands. That does not map, and hedging it as "adjourning or transforming" only papers over the mismatch. The honest reading is that Tuckman covers four of HAIMM's five stages. What happens at the fifth is that a team deliberately re-enters forming and storming, because AI capability changed and the division of labour agreed at Optimization no longer describes the work.

Satir's new status quo is a terminal state: stability re-established at a higher level of performance. A stage called Continuous Evolution cannot be a terminal state, so the lens describes the wrong shape.

What replaces both is the team's own capacity to re-open a settled question. Ethics, which borrows no developmental model at all by design, is the one dimension whose account of this stage does not depend on a lens that has run out.

## The characteristic stall

Stability mistaken for maturity. An ethics policy last substantively edited at the Integration gate, now covering use cases invented well after it. A context artifact that is current because nothing has challenged it. A workflow that has not been questioned since it started working.

Each is indistinguishable from a healthy steady state on any single inspection, and separable only by asking what changed and when.

## Entering the stage

Per dimension, by passing that dimension's Optimization to Continuous Evolution gate. The four gates are in the dimension files under `framework/dimensions/`, and every one of them is marked provisional.
