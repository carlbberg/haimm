# Exploration

*Stage 1 of five*

**Identifying opportunities, understanding user needs and concerns, and setting initial guardrails.**

Nothing has been committed to yet. The organisation is looking at its own work and asking where AI would fit, which is a very different activity from having AI already and looking for somewhere to put it.

## What is happening

At this stage the unit of work is the question and not the tool. Workflows are mapped, pains and concerns are surfaced, and the first guardrails are written down. What makes this a stage and not a preamble is that it can be done well or badly, and doing it badly is usually invisible until much later, when a pilot succeeds at something nobody needed.

Two things are being established at the same time, and they are easy to confuse. The first is what AI could plausibly do in this context. The second is what this particular team actually needs done. Exploration is finished when those two have been established separately, so that the overlap between them turns out to be a finding, and not an assumption you started with.

## What it is not

- **Not the absence of AI.** A team where everyone uses assistants daily but nothing has been agreed is still in Exploration. Individual use is a fact about individuals, and it says very little about where the organisation sits.
- **Not a procurement stage.** Choosing a tool before the need has been established is the failure this stage exists to prevent, and it tends to produce artifacts that read identically to a well-run Exploration except for the dates on them.
- **Not a gate to pass once.** A team sits in Exploration per dimension, so Ethics can be well past it while Solution Fit has not started.

## How the lenses read it

Tuckman's forming and Satir's late status quo agree here, which they do not do at every stage. Forming describes a group that has come together but has not yet tested itself, where roles are stated, politeness is high and disagreement has not surfaced. Late status quo describes routines that still hold and still feel stable. Both of them say the same thing about Exploration: what looks like alignment has not been tested yet, and you should not read it as agreement.

For Solution Fit, the acceptance lenses put performance expectancy first, meaning whether people believe AI would improve their own work. That belief is a perception held by the person doing the job, and it can be wrong in either direction.

## The characteristic stall

A team stalls in Exploration by producing all the artifacts of the stage without doing the enquiry. A backlog of use cases matched to known AI capabilities, a values statement that nobody would disagree with, and a well-attended kickoff with no concern recorded anywhere will all pass a superficial reading. Each of them is examined in its own dimension's Exploration to Experimentation gate, and each fails for the same underlying reason, which is that nothing in the record could have come out differently.

## Leaving the stage

You leave Exploration one dimension at a time, by passing that dimension's Exploration to Experimentation gate, and the four gates are in the dimension files under `framework/dimensions/`. A team does not leave the stage as a whole, and the scatter that produces across the five dimensions is the assessment result you are looking for.
