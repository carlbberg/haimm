# Exploration

*Stage 1 of five*

**Identifying opportunities, understanding user needs and concerns, and setting initial guardrails.**

Nothing has been committed to yet. The organisation is looking at its own work and asking where AI would fit, which is a different activity from having AI and looking for somewhere to put it.

## What is happening

The unit of work is the question, not the tool. Workflows get mapped, pains and concerns get surfaced, and the first guardrails get written. What makes this a stage rather than a preamble is that it can be done well or badly, and doing it badly is not visible until much later, when a pilot succeeds at something nobody needed.

Two things are being established at once, and they are easy to confuse. One is what AI could plausibly do here. The other is what this team actually needs done. Exploration is finished when those have been established separately, so that the overlap between them is a finding rather than an assumption.

## What it is not

- **Not the absence of AI.** A team where everyone uses assistants daily and nothing has been agreed is in Exploration, not past it. Individual use is not organisational position.
- **Not a procurement stage.** Choosing a tool before the need is established is the failure this stage exists to prevent, and it produces artifacts that read identically to a well-run Exploration except for the dates.
- **Not a gate to pass once.** A team sits in Exploration per dimension. Ethics can be well past it while Solution Fit has not started.

## How the lenses read it

Tuckman's forming and Satir's late status quo agree here, which they do not at every stage. Forming describes a group that has come together but not yet tested itself: roles are stated, politeness is high, and disagreement has not surfaced. Late status quo describes routines that still hold and feel stable. Both say the same thing about Exploration: what looks like alignment has not been tested yet, and should not be read as agreement.

For Solution Fit, the acceptance lenses put performance expectancy first: whether people believe AI would improve their work. That belief is a perception held by the person doing the job, and it can be wrong in either direction.

## The characteristic stall

A team stalls in Exploration by producing the artifacts of the stage without the enquiry. A backlog of use cases matched to known AI capabilities, a values statement nobody would disagree with, and a well-attended kickoff with no recorded concern all pass a superficial reading. Each is examined in its own dimension's Exploration to Experimentation gate, and each fails for the same underlying reason: nothing in the record could have come out differently.

## Leaving the stage

Per dimension, by passing that dimension's Exploration to Experimentation gate. The four gates are in the dimension files under `framework/dimensions/`. A team does not leave Exploration as a whole; it leaves it in one dimension at a time, and the scatter that produces is the assessment result.
