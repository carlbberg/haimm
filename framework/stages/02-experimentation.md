# Experimentation

*Stage 2 of five*

**Piloting AI tools at small scale to validate relevance and effectiveness.**

This is the first stage where something is genuinely at stake. A pilot makes an abstract question concrete, and concrete questions tend to produce disagreement.

## What is happening

Small pilots test whether what Exploration established survives contact with real work. The word doing the work is *validate*. A pilot that could not have failed was a demonstration, not a test.

This is also where the change stops being hypothetical for the people it affects. The technical test and the human one run at the same time, and teams routinely confuse them. In other words, a pilot people resist is not a bad pilot for that reason alone, and a pilot people welcome is not a good one for that reason alone.

## What it is not

- **Not a small Integration.** The pilot's job is to produce information, including the kind that stops it. Run a pilot to build the case for a decision somebody already took and you have Integration with a rehearsal attached to the front.
- **Not measured by satisfaction.** Satisfaction describes the experience of using the thing. Whether the thing needed to exist at all belongs to Solution Fit, and it is quite possible to score well on the first while failing the second.
- **Not finished when the pilot ends.** It is finished when the pilot changed something, or when the decision not to change anything was recorded together with the reason for it.

## How the lenses read it

Tuckman's storming and Satir's resistance both fall here, and both make the same unusual prediction. Things get worse before they get better, and the worsening is your evidence that something real is underway. Storming is what happens when expectations collide once roles turn concrete. Resistance is the reaction to a disruption that has stopped being theoretical.

The consequence for assessment is counterintuitive, so let us state it plainly. At this stage, a smooth passage is weak evidence. A pilot with universal enthusiasm and no recorded disagreement either suppressed the conflict or has not yet touched anything anyone cares about. Two dimensions test this from different directions. Human-AI Collaboration asks for a specific disagreement and how the team settled it, and People asks whether a sceptic's objection changed anything.

## The characteristic stall

The first stall is the polite pilot. It ran cleanly, scored well and shipped unchanged. Every artifact of a successful experiment is there, and the experiment itself had no result. The same profile shows up in Ethics as a review checklist somebody fills in identically every time and that has never caused a change.

A team can also stall by never leaving pilot at all. Continuous experimentation with nothing embedded reads as caution. Often it is an unwillingness to commit to a decision the pilots already made.

## Leaving the stage

You leave Experimentation one dimension at a time, by passing that dimension's Experimentation to Integration gate, and the four gates are in the dimension files under `framework/dimensions/`. Ethics carries the provenance criterion at this gate, which asks whether the team can say what grounded an AI-influenced decision.
