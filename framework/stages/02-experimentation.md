# Experimentation

*Stage 2 of five*

**Piloting AI tools at small scale to validate relevance and effectiveness.**

This is the first stage where something is genuinely at stake. A pilot makes an abstract question concrete, and concrete questions tend to produce disagreement.

## What is happening

Small pilots test whether the thing established during Exploration survives contact with real work. The word doing the work here is *validate*, because a pilot that could not have failed was a demonstration and not a test.

This is also the stage where the change stops being hypothetical for the people it affects. Both of those things, the technical test and the human one, run at the same time, and they are usually confused with each other. In other words, a pilot that people resist is not a bad pilot for that reason alone, and a pilot people welcome is not a good one for that reason alone.

## What it is not

- **Not a small Integration.** The pilot's job is to produce information, including the kind of information that stops it. A pilot run to build the case for a decision that has already been taken is Integration with a rehearsal attached to the front of it.
- **Not measured by satisfaction.** Satisfaction describes the experience of using the thing. Whether the thing needed to exist at all belongs to Solution Fit, and it is quite possible to score well on the first while failing the second.
- **Not finished when the pilot ends.** It is finished when the pilot changed something, or when the decision not to change anything was recorded together with the reason for it.

## How the lenses read it

Tuckman's storming and Satir's resistance both fall here, and both make the same unusual prediction, which is that things get worse before they get better and the worsening is the evidence that something real is underway. Storming is what happens when expectations collide once roles become concrete. Resistance is the reaction to a disruption that has stopped being theoretical.

The consequence for assessment is counterintuitive, so it is worth stating plainly: at this stage a smooth passage is weak evidence. A pilot with universal enthusiasm and no recorded disagreement has either suppressed the conflict or has not yet touched anything that anyone cares about. Two dimensions test this from different directions. Human-AI Collaboration asks for a specific disagreement and how it was resolved, and People asks whether a sceptic's objection actually changed anything.

## The characteristic stall

The first stall is the polite pilot. It ran cleanly, it scored well, and it shipped unchanged, so that every artifact of a successful experiment is present while the experiment itself had no result. The same profile shows up in Ethics as a review checklist that is always filled in identically and has never once caused a change.

A team can also stall by never leaving pilot at all. Continuous experimentation with nothing embedded reads as caution, and it is often an unwillingness to commit to a decision that the pilots have in fact already made.

## Leaving the stage

You leave Experimentation one dimension at a time, by passing that dimension's Experimentation to Integration gate, and the four gates are in the dimension files under `framework/dimensions/`. Ethics carries the provenance criterion at this gate, which asks whether the team can say what grounded an AI-influenced decision.
