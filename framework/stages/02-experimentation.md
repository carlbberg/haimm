# Experimentation

*Stage 2 of five*

**Piloting AI tools at small scale to validate relevance and effectiveness.**

The first stage where something is at stake. A pilot makes an abstract question concrete, and concrete questions produce disagreement.

## What is happening

Small pilots test whether the thing established in Exploration survives contact with real work. The word doing the work is *validate*: a pilot that could not have failed was a demonstration, not a test.

This is also the stage where the change stops being hypothetical for the people affected by it. Both of those, the technical test and the human one, run at the same time and are usually confused with each other. A pilot that people resist is not thereby a bad pilot, and a pilot people welcome is not thereby a good one.

## What it is not

- **Not a small Integration.** The pilot's job is to produce information, including information that stops it. A pilot run to build the case for a decision already taken is Integration with a rehearsal attached.
- **Not measured by satisfaction.** Satisfaction describes the experience of using the thing. Whether the thing needed to exist is Solution Fit, and it is possible to score well on the first while failing the second.
- **Not finished when the pilot ends.** It is finished when the pilot changed something, or when the decision not to change was recorded with a reason.

## How the lenses read it

Tuckman's storming and Satir's resistance both fall here, and both make the same unusual prediction: things get worse before they get better, and the worsening is the evidence that something real is underway. Storming is expectations colliding once roles become concrete. Resistance is the reaction to a disruption that has stopped being theoretical.

The consequence for assessment is counterintuitive and worth stating plainly. At this stage a smooth passage is weak evidence. A pilot with universal enthusiasm and no recorded disagreement has either suppressed the conflict or has not yet touched anything anyone cares about. Two dimensions test exactly this from different directions: Human-AI Collaboration asks for a specific disagreement and its resolution, and People asks whether a sceptic's objection changed anything.

## The characteristic stall

The polite pilot. It ran cleanly, scored well, and shipped unchanged. Every artifact of a successful experiment is present and the experiment had no result. The same profile appears in Ethics as a review checklist that is always filled in identically and has never once caused a change.

A team can also stall by never leaving pilot. Continuous experimentation with nothing embedded reads as caution and is often an unwillingness to commit to a decision the pilots have already made.

## Leaving the stage

Per dimension, by passing that dimension's Experimentation to Integration gate. The four gates are in the dimension files under `framework/dimensions/`. Ethics carries the provenance criterion at this gate: whether the team can say what grounded an AI-influenced decision.
