# Optimization

*Stage 4 of five*

**Scaling what worked, focused on measurable outcomes: productivity, quality, learning.**

This is the first stage whose claim is empirical. You can evidence every earlier stage by how the work was done, whereas this one claims the work actually produced something, which is a harder thing to show.

## What is happening

What the team embedded in one context now runs beyond it. Somebody designed the oversight instead of improvising it, context comes from sources of record instead of copies passed between people, and somebody measures the impact claim instead of asserting it.

The real test is transferability. The setup behind a working pilot is often a fit between AI and one team's accumulated context, not between AI and the workflow, and you cannot tell the two apart until somebody else tries it. A second team reaching working value from documented setup alone is the cleanest evidence anywhere in the model. It is also the criterion people skip most often, for logistical reasons.

## What it is not

- **Not a metrics threshold.** No metric is an entry condition for any stage, here or anywhere else. Gates place a team and metrics say whether it is moving. The reasoning is Campbell's law: a measure that decides a placement stops measuring the thing somebody chose it for. See `framework/metrics.md`.
- **Not headcount.** Wide usage across an organisation, with the underlying capability still sitting in one team, is Integration reported at Optimization's scale.
- **Not stability.** Running well is the achievement of this stage, and it is the trap of the next one.

## How the lenses read it

Tuckman's performing and Satir's integration both fall here, and for once they agree. Performing describes a group working cohesively with minimal friction. Satir's integration is where new practice becomes routine and performance rises past the level it held before the change began.

Treat that agreement carefully, however. Both lenses describe a state that feels settled, and both set out to describe groups moving toward a resting point. HAIMM's fifth stage denies there is one. That is where both lenses stop being useful.

## The characteristic stall

The first stall is measurement standing in for the thing it measures. You see a dashboard of AI usage and satisfaction, rising steadily, with no record anywhere of an outcome that changed. Usage tells you people opened the tool. It does not tell you that opening it was worth their time.

The second stall is governance that was accurate at launch. The documentation for a decision-making system describes what it did on the day it went live, nobody has touched it since, and the system moved underneath it. It passes an existence check and fails a currency one. Currency is what the Ethics gate at this transition asks about.

## Leaving the stage

You leave Optimization one dimension at a time, by passing that dimension's Optimization to Continuous Evolution gate. The four gates live in the dimension files under `framework/dimensions/`. Those four are the least trustworthy criteria in the model. No assessment has yet placed a team past Optimization on any dimension, so they are reasoned and not observed. There is more on this in `05-continuous-evolution.md`.
