# Optimization

*Stage 4 of five*

**Scaling what worked, focused on measurable outcomes: productivity, quality, learning.**

This is the first stage whose claim is empirical. Everything before it can be evidenced by how the work was done, whereas this one asserts that the work actually produced something.

## What is happening

What was embedded in one context now runs beyond it. Oversight is designed instead of improvised, context is referenced from sources of record instead of copied between people, and the impact claim is measured instead of asserted.

The real test is transferability. The setup that made a pilot work is often a fit between AI and one team's accumulated context, not a fit between AI and the workflow, and the two are indistinguishable until somebody else tries it. A second team reaching working value from documented setup alone is the cleanest evidence anywhere in the model, and it is also the criterion most often skipped for logistical reasons.

## What it is not

- **Not a metrics threshold.** No metric is an entry condition for any stage, here or anywhere else. Gates place a team and metrics say whether it is moving, and the reasoning is Campbell's law: a measure that decides a placement stops measuring the thing it was chosen for. See `framework/metrics.md`.
- **Not headcount.** Wide usage across an organisation, where the underlying capability is still held by one team, is Integration being reported at Optimization's scale.
- **Not stability.** Running well is the achievement of this stage, and it is the trap of the next one.

## How the lenses read it

Tuckman's performing and Satir's integration both fall here, and for once they agree. Performing describes a group working cohesively with minimal friction. Satir's integration is where new practice becomes routine and performance rises past the level it held before the change began.

That agreement is worth treating carefully, however. Both lenses describe a state that feels settled, and both were built to describe groups moving toward a resting point. HAIMM's fifth stage denies that there is one, which is the point where both lenses stop being useful.

## The characteristic stall

The first stall is measurement substituted for the thing being measured. You see a dashboard of AI usage and satisfaction, rising steadily, with no record anywhere of an outcome that changed. Usage tells you that people opened the tool, and it does not tell you that opening it was worth their time.

The second stall is governance that was accurate at launch. The documentation for a decision-making system describes what it did on the day it went live and has not been touched since, while the system itself changed underneath it. It passes an existence check and fails a currency one, and currency is what the Ethics gate at this transition asks about.

## Leaving the stage

You leave Optimization one dimension at a time, by passing that dimension's Optimization to Continuous Evolution gate, and the four gates are in the dimension files under `framework/dimensions/`. Those four are the least trustworthy criteria in the model, because no assessment has yet placed a team past Optimization on any dimension, which means they are reasoned and not observed. There is more on this in `05-continuous-evolution.md`.
