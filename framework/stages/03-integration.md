# Integration

*Stage 3 of five*

**Embedding AI into workflows and establishing working norms between teams and AI tools.**

The stage where the two developmental lenses HAIMM borrows contradict each other, and where the contradiction is the point.

## What is happening

What worked in a pilot becomes part of how the work is done. Access, permissions and tooling are arranged so that using AI is no longer an act of initiative. Norms appear for what AI contributes and what a person still owns, and context starts moving from individual heads into something the team holds.

The load-bearing word is *embedding*. A tool available to everyone and used at each person's discretion has not been integrated. It has been distributed.

## What it is not

- **Not Satir's integration phase.** The two share a word and do not line up. Satir's integration, where new practice settles and performance recovers, falls at HAIMM's Optimization. Satir's chaos falls here. The collision is worth naming every time the two are used together.
- **Not scale.** Integration is depth in one context. Whether it survives being handed to a second team is Optimization, and assuming the two arrive together is the most common overstatement of a result.
- **Not the end of disagreement.** Norms are what a team does with disagreement, not evidence that it stopped.

## How the lenses read it

Tuckman says norming: the group settles, trust builds, and collaboration gets smoother. Satir says chaos: roles shift, stability drops, and people temporarily perform worse than they did before.

Both are correct, about different objects. The working agreement between people and AI is settling, which is norming. The routines and role boundaries that agreement displaces are coming apart, which is chaos. A team can be norming with AI and in chaos about itself at the same time, and usually is.

That is the most useful thing this stage offers an assessor, because it predicts a specific distortion. The Human-AI Collaboration reading will look better than the People reading, the gap is real rather than an inconsistency to reconcile, and reading only one of them produces the wrong diagnosis. It also explains why capability concentrates here: when routines are unsettled, the people who build their own workarounds fastest pull ahead, and the distribution criterion in the People dimension exists to catch that.

## The characteristic stall

Integration achieved in the workflow and not in the people. The process is rebuilt, the documentation exists, and the capability to run it sits with whoever adapted first. It passes every artifact check and fails at the moment that person leaves or the workload doubles.

The second stall is grounding. AI treated as a colleague while every session starts from a blank slate, which is trust without sufficiency, and a well-liked tool that has not actually graduated.

## Leaving the stage

Per dimension, by passing that dimension's Integration to Optimization gate. The four gates are in the dimension files under `framework/dimensions/`. Three of the four dimension-interaction criteria sit at this gate: transferability in Solution Fit, sufficiency in Human-AI Collaboration, and distribution in People. That concentration is not accidental. This is the stage whose achievements are most often local, and the transition out of it is where local stops being enough.
