# Running an assessment

Places a team at a stage on each dimension, using evidence rather than self-perception. Output is a profile, usually uneven, and the unevenness is the finding.

Only Knowledge & Context has gates drafted. Until the other sixteen exist, this runs as a single-dimension assessment.

## What you need

- Access to the team's shared documents and instruction files, including edit history. Most of the artifact criteria depend on last-changed dates and author lists.
- One or two hours of observed work. Someone doing real AI-assisted work while you watch, not a demo.
- Twenty minutes each with three or four people in different roles.

Without artifact access, the assessment degrades to self-report and will overrate the team. Say so in the write-up rather than quietly producing a number.

## Sequence

**1. Survey first, to scope.** Send `instruments/survey-knowledge-context.md`. It covers the [S] criteria only. Treat the results as a map of where to look, not as findings. A team that answers yes to everything is telling you where to be sceptical.

**2. Inspect artifacts.** Work the [A] criteria. This is where most of the real signal is, because dates and author lists are hard to argue with. Record what you found, not whether you ticked the box.

**3. Observe.** Work the [O] criteria in a real session. The most useful moment is when someone supplies context: watch whether they reach for a shared source or reconstruct it from memory. Ask afterwards where it came from.

**4. Place.** Fill in `instruments/checklist-knowledge-context.md`. A team is at the highest stage whose entry gate it passed. Pass is three of four criteria, including the core one.

**5. Read the failure signals before writing anything up.** Each gate names the profile that passes a superficial reading and fails the real one. Check the team against those specifically. A team that looks strong on every artifact criterion and cannot produce a grounding failure is the most common false positive in the instrument.

## Reporting

Report the stage, the gate that stopped them, and the specific criteria that failed. "Integration, held at the Integration to Optimization gate on reachability and independent agreement" is actionable. "Level 3" is not.

Do not average across dimensions or produce a single overall score. It destroys the only useful property of the profile, which is that it points at one thing to fix next.

## Known weaknesses

Say these out loud rather than letting them sit under a confident-looking result.

- **Gate 4 is untested.** No team in the v0.1 worked example was plotted at Continuous Evolution on any dimension. Its criteria are reasoned, not observed.
- **The independent-agreement test is awkward to run.** Getting two people and an AI session to answer the same question properly takes setup. It is marked core precisely because it is the one people skip, but that does not make it easy.
- **Observation changes behaviour.** People supply better context when watched. Where possible, look at what was actually pasted into recent sessions rather than relying only on live observation.
- **One team is not the organisation.** Placement is per team. Rolling several teams into an organisational score reintroduces the averaging problem.
