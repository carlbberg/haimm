# Acting on a result

An assessment that names a gap and then offers no next move is only half a tool, and this file is the other half of it.

Nothing here has been validated against practice. The profiles below are reasoned from the model's own structure and have not been observed in any population, so treat them as a way of reading a result and not as findings in their own right.

## The one rule

**Work the failed criterion, not the stage.**

Gate criteria are written as states that can be inspected, which means that each of them already contains its own next move. A team held at a gate on "every context artifact the team relies on has a named owner and a last substantive change inside an agreed freshness window" does not have a Knowledge & Context problem to solve. What it has is a list of artifacts with no owner, and the task in front of it is to give them one.

"Get to Integration" is not a plan, whereas "these four documents have no owner and none of them has changed in eight months" is one.

## Sequencing

**Work one gate at a time, across the whole profile,** rather than one gate per dimension in parallel. A team that tries to move five dimensions at once tends to move none of them, and in any case the dimensions do not need to move together.

**Pick the gate by consequence and not by depth.** The lowest-scoring dimension is not automatically the one to work on. Ask instead which failure is currently costing the team something and which one is compounding. A dimension sitting at Exploration that nothing depends on yet can wait, whereas one at Integration that everything routes through cannot.

**Reassess after a cycle, not continuously.** Gates measure states that usually take a quarter or more to change, so running the instrument every month will mostly measure the assessor's mood.

## Reading the shape

There are six profiles worth learning to recognise, and the pairwise ones matter considerably more than any single dimension's level.

**Solution Fit ahead, Knowledge & Context behind.** The tools fit the work and the grounding lives in a few people's heads. This usually presents itself as "our pilot worked and the rollout stalled". The move is transferability, which means documenting what made the pilot work before scaling anything else. Attacking it as a training or tooling problem is the standard mistake here, and an expensive one.

**Human-AI Collaboration ahead of Knowledge & Context.** Trust has outrun grounding. The team treats AI as a colleague and re-briefs it at the start of every session, which is expensive and invisible at the same time, because it feels like normal work. This is the gap that the row order was chosen to make visible, since the two dimensions sit adjacent to each other in the assessment view. The move is reachability, and it is not more trust.

**People behind everything else.** Capability has concentrated. One or two people carry the AI-supported workflow, and the profile looks healthy largely because they are the people who got interviewed. The move is distribution, meaning onboarding material and a named person who is not the original builder running the workflow unaided.

**Ethics behind everything else.** The risk compounds as the automation level rises, so this profile gets worse over time without anything else changing. A team at Optimization on Human-AI Collaboration and Exploration on Ethics is running semi-autonomous operation behind unfalsifiable guardrails. The move is a single specific guardrail that names an action it prohibits, applied to the highest-automation use case currently in play.

**Flat and low.** All five dimensions sit at Exploration or Experimentation. This is the normal state of a team that started recently, and it is not a crisis. Work Solution Fit and Knowledge & Context first, because the other three have much less to bite on until there is something real in use.

**Flat and high.** All five dimensions sit at Optimization or above. Check the evidence carefully before you believe it, because a uniformly high profile from a survey-led assessment with thin artifact access is the instrument's most likely false positive. The failure signals are your check here: a team that cannot produce a single grounding failure, a pilot that never changed, and an ethics checklist that has never blocked anything.

## What not to do

- **Do not level the profile.** Even placement across the five dimensions is not a goal in itself. The scatter is information, and a team that flattens it has usually optimised the assessment instead of the work.
- **Do not treat the stages as a roadmap with dates attached.** They describe states and not a schedule, and a team can quite reasonably sit at Integration on a dimension for years because that is the right place for it to be.
- **Do not publish the criteria to the team before you assess them.** Published criteria get optimised against. If you are going to share them, share them after placement, as a description of what to do next, which is what they are genuinely good for.
- **Do not convert the profile into a number for reporting upward.** If somebody demands a single figure, give them the gate that is blocking and what it would take to pass it instead. That answer is usually shorter than a number would be, and unlike a number it tells them something they can act on.

## Where this stops

None of this has met a real team yet, and that limit sits behind every profile above. The reading of a result will change once the gates have been run in anger, and the profiles most likely to change are the pairwise ones, because they are the furthest from anything the criteria can be checked against directly.

So it is worth ending on the questions rather than on the answers. When you look at your own team's profile, ask yourself: is the AI you have adopted attached to a need somebody actually has? Would a second team get anywhere with it? And if the answer to one of those is uncomfortable, is that discomfort recorded anywhere a decision can reach it? Those are better starting points than a level, and they are what the whole instrument is trying to get you to.
