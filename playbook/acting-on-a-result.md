# Acting on a result

An assessment that names a gap and then offers no next move is only half a tool, and this file is the other half.

None of this has been validated against practice. We reasoned the profiles below from the model's own structure and have not observed them in any population, so treat them as a way of reading a result and not as findings.

## The one rule

**Work the failed criterion, not the stage.**

Gate criteria describe states you can inspect, so each one already contains its own next move. A team held at a gate on "every context artifact the team relies on has a named owner and a last substantive change inside an agreed freshness window" does not have a Knowledge & Context problem to solve. It has a list of artifacts with no owner. The task is to give them one.

"Get to Integration" is not a plan, whereas "these four documents have no owner and none of them has changed in eight months" is one.

## Sequencing

**Work one gate at a time, across the whole profile,** not one gate per dimension in parallel. A team that tries to move five dimensions at once tends to move none of them, and the dimensions do not need to move together anyway.

**Pick the gate by consequence and not by depth.** The lowest-scoring dimension is not automatically the one to work. Ask three questions instead. Which failure is costing the team something right now? Which one is compounding? And what would it actually take to pass that gate? A dimension at Exploration that nothing depends on yet can wait. One at Integration that everything routes through cannot.

**Reassess after a cycle, not continuously.** Gates measure states that usually take a quarter or more to change. Run the instrument every month and you will mostly measure your own mood.

## Reading the shape

Six profiles are worth learning to recognise. The pairwise ones matter considerably more than any single dimension's level.

**Solution Fit ahead, Knowledge & Context behind.** The tools fit the work and the grounding lives in a few people's heads. It usually presents as "our pilot worked and the rollout stalled". The move is transferability: document what made the pilot work before scaling anything else. Attacking it as a training or tooling problem is the standard mistake, and an expensive one.

**Human-AI Collaboration ahead of Knowledge & Context.** Trust has outrun grounding. The team treats AI as a colleague and re-briefs it at the start of every session, which is expensive and invisible at once, because it feels like normal work. We put the two dimensions on adjacent rows to make exactly this gap visible. The move is reachability. It is not more trust.

**People behind everything else.** Capability has pooled. One or two people carry the AI-supported workflow, and the profile looks healthy largely because those are the people you happened to interview. The move is distribution: onboarding material, and a named person who is not the original builder running the workflow unaided.

**Ethics behind everything else.** The risk compounds as automation rises, so this profile gets worse over time with nothing else changing. A team at Optimization on Human-AI Collaboration and Exploration on Ethics is running semi-autonomous operation behind unfalsifiable guardrails. The move is one specific guardrail naming an action it prohibits, applied to the highest-automation use case in play.

**Flat and low.** All five dimensions sit at Exploration or Experimentation. This is the normal state of a team that started recently. It is not a crisis. Work Solution Fit and Knowledge & Context first, because the other three have less to bite on until something real is in use.

**Flat and high.** All five dimensions sit at Optimization or above. Check the evidence before you believe it, because a uniformly high profile from a survey-led assessment with thin artifact access is this instrument's most likely false positive. The failure signals are your check: a team that cannot produce a single grounding failure, a pilot that never changed, an ethics checklist that has never blocked anything.

## What not to do

- **Do not level the profile.** Even placement across the five dimensions is not a goal. The scatter is information, and a team that flattens it has usually optimised the assessment instead of the work.
- **Do not treat the stages as a roadmap with dates attached.** They describe states, not a schedule. A team can quite reasonably sit at Integration on a dimension for years, because that is the right place for it.
- **Do not publish the criteria to the team before you assess them.** Published criteria get optimised against. Share them after placement, as a description of what to do next. That is what they are genuinely good for.
- **Do not convert the profile into a number for reporting upward.** If somebody demands a single figure, give them the blocking gate and what it would take to pass it. That answer is usually shorter than a number, and unlike a number it tells them something they can act on.

## Where this stops

None of this has met a real team yet, and that limit sits behind every profile above. How we read a result will change once somebody runs the gates in anger. The pairwise profiles will move most, because they sit furthest from anything the criteria check directly.

So let us end on the questions instead of the answers. Look at your own team's profile and ask: is the AI you adopted attached to a need somebody actually has? Would a second team get anywhere with it? And if one of those answers is uncomfortable, is that discomfort written down anywhere a decision can reach it? Those are better starting points than a level, and they are what the whole instrument is trying to get you to.
